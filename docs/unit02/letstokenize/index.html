🧬 Byte Pair Encoding — Watch It Learn

The subword algorithm behind GPT-style tokenizers. No dictionary required — it invents its own vocabulary by merging the most frequent pair, one step at a time.

Why bother with subwords?

Storing every whole word as its own token gets expensive fast, and any word the model hasn't seen before becomes a dead end. BPE solves both problems by learning small, reusable pieces instead.

  • ✂️Instead of one giant token for a word like "newer", it learns tiny reusable chunks — things like ne, new, low, er, est — and builds words back up from them.
  • 🧩A brand-new or rare word can still be assembled from pieces the model already knows, instead of collapsing into one big "unknown" token.
  • 📉Fewer, more reusable pieces means a smaller vocabulary and shorter token sequences overall — which is exactly why real tokenizers (GPT, LLaMA, etc.) all use some flavor of this.

Every word starts as loose characters. Each step counts every adjacent pair across the whole corpus (weighted by how often each word shows up), welds the single most frequent pair into one new symbol, and does it again — everywhere that pair appears, all at once.
